Crispy Japanese tempura of prawns and broccoli. Serve with either one or both dipping sauces: a warm sweet-salty miso dip (great with the broccoli) and a fresh citrus-sesame dip (perfect with the prawns).

## Components

### Warm Sweet-Salty Miso Dip
slug: warm-miso-dip
prepTime: 5
cookTime: 2

#### Ingredients

- 1.5 tbsp miso paste {scale}
- 2 tbsp sake {scale}
- 2 tbsp mirin {scale}
- 1 tsp soy sauce {scale}
- A few drops of sesame oil

#### Instructions

1. Bring the sake and mirin to a boil in a small saucepan for 30 seconds to burn off the alcohol.
2. Reduce the heat to low, whisk in the miso and soy sauce until smooth, and let it thicken gently for 1 minute.
3. Remove from the heat and stir in a few drops of sesame oil. Serve warm or lukewarm (this earthy umami dip pairs wonderfully with the broccoli).

### Fresh Citrus-Sesame Dip
slug: citrus-sesame-dip
prepTime: 5

#### Ingredients

- 2 tbsp soy sauce {scale}
- 1 tbsp sushi vinegar {scale}
- 1 tbsp fresh lime juice {scale}
- 1 tbsp mirin {scale}
- 0.5 tbsp sake {scale}
- 1 tsp sesame oil {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Whisk all ingredients together cold in a small bowl until the sesame oil is evenly emulsified (this bright, tangy dip is a perfect match for the prawns).

### Tempura Batter (Base Recipe)
slug: tempura-batter
prepTime: 2

#### Ingredients

- 30 g flour (spelt flour or all-purpose wheat flour) {scale}
- 20 g cornstarch {scale}
- 75 ml ice-cold sparkling water (almost at freezing point) {scale}
- 1 ice cube {scale}
- 1 small pinch of salt

#### Instructions

1. Prepare the batter at the very last moment, only when the frying oil is up to temperature and the broccoli and prawns are completely prepped.
2. Place the flour, cornstarch, salt, and the ice cube into a bowl.
3. Pour in the ice-cold sparkling water.
4. Using chopsticks or a fork, stir very loosely for just 5 to a maximum of 10 seconds. Stop immediately, even if lumps and streaks of dry flour remain. Never whisk until smooth.

### Broccoli Tempura
prepTime: 10
cookTime: 5

#### Ingredients

- 12 small broccoli florets (approx. 2.5 to 3 cm wide) {scale}
- 1 tsp cornstarch or flour (for dusting) {scale}
- Neutral frying oil (e.g., sunflower or peanut oil)
- Salt to taste

#### Instructions

1. Cut {{12}} small broccoli florets about 2.5 to 3 cm wide, leaving 1.5 cm of stem attached as a handle.
2. Blanch the florets for exactly 30 to 45 seconds in salted boiling water.
3. Immediately rinse under cold water and pat completely dry in a clean kitchen towel. Spread them out so no residual steam or moisture remains.
4. Lightly dust the florets with a teaspoon of cornstarch or flour and shake off any excess powder.
5. Heat the oil to 180°C (350°F). Hold a floret by its stem, dip it into the ice-cold tempura batter, and let any excess drip off briefly.
6. Gently lower into the hot oil. Fry a maximum of 4 to 5 florets at a time so the oil temperature does not drop.
7. Fry for 2 to 3 minutes until the batter is crisp, airy, and pale golden yellow.
8. Lift out with a slotted spoon, drain immediately on a wire rack, and season right away with a pinch of salt.

### Prawn Tempura (Nobashi Ebi)
prepTime: 10
cookTime: 4

#### Ingredients

- 6 large raw prawns (shell-on, tail-on) {scale}
- 1 tsp cornstarch (for dusting) {scale}
- Salt to taste

#### Instructions

1. Peel the {{6}} prawns, leaving the very last tail segment attached. Devein the prawns.
2. Trim the sharp tip of the tail fin at an angle and scrape the back of your knife along the tail to press out any trapped moisture (this prevents oil splatter during frying).
3. Lay each prawn belly-side up and make 4 to 5 diagonal cuts halfway through the thickness of the flesh.
4. Flip the prawn over (back-side up on the cutting board). Press down firmly with your thumbs along the cut areas while gently stretching the prawn lengthwise with your fingers until you feel or hear distinct little "snaps". This breaks the muscle fibers so the prawn stays straight and relaxed during frying (Nobashi Ebi).
5. Pat the prawns bone dry with paper towels and dust them very lightly with cornstarch (keep the tail fin clean).
6. Keep the oil at 180°C (350°F). Hold a prawn by the tail fin, dip the body into the cold batter, and gently lower it into the hot oil.
7. Fry for 1.5 to a maximum of 2 minutes. Remove as soon as the batter is crisp and pale yellow; overcooking will make the prawn meat rubbery.
8. Drain on a wire rack, season lightly with salt, and serve immediately.

## Notes

- The secret to lacy tempura: using ice-cold sparkling water and barely stirring the batter prevents gluten development. The thermal shock between the ice-cold batter and the hot oil (180°C), combined with carbonation, creates rapid steam release and a delicate, shatteringly crisp crust.
- Always drain fried tempura on a wire rack rather than paper towels; paper traps steam underneath and makes the bottom soggy.
- The warm, savoury miso dip complements the roasted, earthy notes of the broccoli, while the bright citrus-sesame dip cuts through the richness of the fried prawns.
