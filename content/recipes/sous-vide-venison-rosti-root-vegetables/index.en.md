A refined game dish with pink sous-vide venison steak, crisp potato rösti, roasted vegetables, a rich game jus and toasted hazelnuts.

## Components

### Sous-vide venison steak

#### Ingredients

- 200 g venison steak (100 g per person) {scale}
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil {scale}
- 10 g butter {scale}
- 1 sprig thyme {scale}
- 1 crushed clove garlic {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Preheat the sous-vide water bath to 53 °C.
2. Season the venison lightly with salt and vacuum-seal.
3. Cook for 60 minutes at 53 °C. This is suitable for a steak about 2–3 cm thick, provided the meat reaches the target temperature throughout.
4. Remove the meat from the bag and pat very dry.
5. Just before serving, heat the oil in a frying pan until very hot.
6. Sear the steak for about 30–45 seconds per side to form a brown crust.
7. Add the butter, thyme and garlic towards the end and baste briefly.
8. Cut into neat slices just before serving.

### Oven-baked potato rösti

#### Ingredients

- 250 g potatoes {scale}
- 1 tbsp melted butter or oil {scale}
- Salt and black pepper

#### Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 200 °C (190 °C fan).
2. Peel the potatoes and coarsely grate them.
3. Squeeze out as much moisture as possible using a clean tea towel.
4. Mix with the melted butter or oil, salt and pepper.
5. Form 2 compact rösti on a baking tray lined with baking paper.
6. Bake for about 30 minutes.
7. Turn the rösti after 15 minutes so both sides become golden and crisp.

### Roasted carrots and parsnips

#### Ingredients

- 4 carrots (2 per person) {scale}
- 200 g parsnips {scale}
- 1–2 tbsp olive oil {scale}
- Salt and pepper
- A sprig of thyme, optional

#### Instructions

1. Peel the carrots and parsnips.
2. Leave small carrots whole if desired. Halve larger carrots lengthways.
3. Cut the parsnips into evenly sized long pieces.
4. Toss the vegetables with olive oil, salt, pepper and optional thyme.
5. Spread over a baking tray, leaving plenty of space between the pieces.
6. Roast for about 25–35 minutes at 200 °C, depending on thickness.
7. Turn halfway through. They should caramelise lightly while becoming tender inside.

### Game jus

#### Ingredients

- 200 ml game stock {scale}
- 75 ml red wine {scale}
- 1 small shallot, finely diced {scale}
- 1 small sprig thyme {scale}
- 1 lightly crushed juniper berry {scale}
- 10 g cold butter {scale}
- A little butter for frying
- 1 tsp redcurrant jelly, optional {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Gently fry the finely diced shallot in a little butter.
2. Add the red wine, thyme and crushed juniper berry.
3. Reduce the wine by about half.
4. Add the game stock.
5. Simmer until reduced to about 100–125 ml, or until the jus has sufficient flavour and concentration.
6. Strain the jus.
7. Add a small teaspoon of redcurrant jelly if desired for a subtle fruity note.
8. Just before serving, remove the pan from the heat and whisk the cold butter into the jus for gloss and a little body.
9. Taste and season with salt and pepper if needed.

### Hazelnuts and plating

#### Ingredients

- 15–20 g hazelnuts {scale}
- A few small sprigs of fresh thyme, optional

#### Instructions

1. Roughly chop the hazelnuts.
2. Toast in a dry frying pan over medium heat until golden and fragrant.
3. Allow to cool and set aside until plating.
4. Place one crisp potato rösti slightly off centre on each warm plate.
5. Arrange the carrots and parsnips neatly beside the rösti.
6. Slice the venison on the diagonal and arrange the slices slightly overlapping.
7. Spoon a little game jus beside the meat, leaving the crust visible.
8. Scatter the toasted hazelnuts mainly over the vegetables.
9. Finish with a small sprig of thyme if desired.

## Notes

- Timing, 60 minutes before serving: put the venison in the sous-vide bath at 53 °C; prepare the game jus.
- Timing, 35 minutes before serving: preheat the oven; prepare the carrots and parsnips.
- Timing, 30 minutes before serving: put the rösti and vegetables in the oven.
- Timing, 15 minutes before serving: turn the rösti and vegetables; check and reduce the jus further if needed; toast the hazelnuts.
- Timing, final 5 minutes: remove the venison from the bath and pat dry; sear briefly over high heat; finish the jus with cold butter; plate and serve immediately.
- 53 °C produces red to pink venison. Since 60 minutes at this temperature does not guarantee pasteurisation, this preparation is primarily suitable for an intact cut of meat that has been carefully chilled and handled. A higher, safe core temperature is appropriate for vulnerable groups.
- Venison is lean: sear very briefly after sous-vide cooking to avoid overcooking.
- Squeeze the grated potatoes thoroughly for crisp rösti.
- Allow the carrots and parsnips to caramelise lightly for natural sweetness.
- The game jus should be concentrated but not too salty. Taste as it reduces.
- Use the hazelnuts sparingly for a subtle crunch.
