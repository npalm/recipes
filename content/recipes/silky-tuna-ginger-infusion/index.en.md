An elegant main course featuring butter-soft sous-vide tuna, served on a velvety orange sweet potato cream with ginger, accompanied by crisp broccoli florets.

## Components

### Ginger Sweet Potato Cream
prepTime: 10
cookTime: 15

#### Ingredients

- 300 g orange sweet potato (in 2 cm cubes) {scale}
- 25 g fresh ginger (thinly sliced) {scale}
- 80 ml full-fat coconut milk {scale}
- Pinch of salt

#### Instructions

1. Peel the ginger easily by scraping the skin off with the edge of a teaspoon; this is the most efficient method for ginger's irregular shapes.

2. Cook the sweet potato cubes with the ginger slices in a shallow layer of water for {{12-15}} minutes until tender. Drain, let steam off well, and remove the ginger.

3. Puree with the coconut milk into a smooth cream. Push through a fine-mesh strainer for a silky texture. Season with salt. Keep in a saucepan, covered with foil.

### Tuna
prepTime: 5
cookTime: 25

#### Ingredients

- 2 tuna steaks (sashimi grade, approx. 1.5 cm thick) {scale}
- Splash of olive oil
- 3 to 5 drops sesame oil
- Splash of neutral oil (for searing)

#### Instructions

1. Pat the steaks dry. Vacuum-seal them with a small drop of olive oil and just 3 to 5 drops of sesame oil. Store in the refrigerator.

2. Submerge the vacuum bag with tuna in a 45°C (113°F) water bath for {{20-25}} minutes.

3. Remove the tuna from the bag and pat extremely dry. Heat a pan with a drop of oil over very high heat. Sear the tuna for only 10 to 15 seconds per side for the crust.

### Broccoli
prepTime: 5
cookTime: 5

#### Ingredients

- 100 g broccoli florets {scale}
- Pinch of salt
- Optional: sesame seeds

#### Instructions

1. Blanch the florets in boiling salted water for {{2}} minutes. Shock immediately in ice water, pat dry, and set aside.

2. Grill or torch the broccoli florets briefly for a nice 'crunch' and a lightly charred flavor.

### Plating and Serving

#### Instructions

1. Gently reheat the sweet potato cream.

2. Pull a clean 'swipe' of the orange cream across the plate.

3. Place the tuna on top and arrange the broccoli around it for a beautiful contrast. Finish with sesame seeds if desired.

## Notes

- Sashimi grade tuna is essential: the core temperature stays at 45°C, so the fish must be safe to eat raw.
- Pat the tuna truly extremely dry before searing, otherwise you will get a grey edge instead of a crust.
- The fine-mesh strainer makes the difference between an 'ordinary' puree and a professional cream; do not skip this step.
