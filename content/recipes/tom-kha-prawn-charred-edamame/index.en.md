A refined Asian soup course featuring sous-vide cooked prawns, an airy Tom Kha emulsion of coconut milk and lemongrass, finished with charred edamame.

## Components

### Prawns
prepTime: 5
cookTime: 30

#### Ingredients

- 4 large raw prawns (peeled and deveined) {scale}
- 1 small piece lemongrass (bruised)
- 10 g butter (or coconut oil) {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Pat the cleaned prawns bone-dry. Place them in a vacuum bag with the bruised piece of lemongrass and the butter. Seal and store in the refrigerator.

2. Submerge the vacuum bag with prawns in a 60°C (140°F) water bath for {{25-30}} minutes.

### Tom Kha Emulsion
prepTime: 5
cookTime: 20

#### Ingredients

- 200 ml full-fat coconut milk {scale}
- 1 stalk lemongrass (coarsely chopped and bruised)
- 2 cm fresh galangal (or ginger), sliced
- 1 tbsp fish sauce {scale}
- 1 tbsp brown sugar {scale}
- Juice of ½ lime {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Bring the coconut milk, lemongrass, galangal, fish sauce, and sugar to a gentle simmer. Let infuse on low heat for {{20}} minutes.

2. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve and set aside.

### Garnish
prepTime: 5

#### Ingredients

- Handful of edamame beans (thawed) {scale}
- Chili oil (for the finishing touch)

#### Instructions

1. Pat the thawed beans bone-dry and toss with a drop of neutral oil.

2. Use a blowtorch to char the edamame beans until they show dark, roasted spots.

### Plating and Serving

#### Instructions

1. Bring the Tom Kha broth to a boil, remove from heat, and stir in the lime juice. Use an immersion blender to froth the soup into a thick, white emulsion.

2. Divide the prawns into black bowls. Pour the hot broth around them and spoon the white foam on top.

3. Garnish with the charred edamame and a drop of chili oil.

## Notes

- Sous-vide at 60°C keeps the prawns translucent and juicy; do not exceed this temperature or they will turn rubbery.
- A chamber vacuum sealer is ideal for the prawns, but a zip-lock bag with the water displacement method works fine for this short cook.
- Galangal has a sharper, more citrusy bite than ginger and is the authentic choice; only substitute ginger if galangal is unavailable.
