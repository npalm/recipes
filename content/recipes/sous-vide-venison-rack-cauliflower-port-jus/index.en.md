A refined main course for two: rosy venison rack with thyme and rosemary, silky cauliflower purée, a rich port and game jus with juniper berries, and two thin, crispy potato rösti with mature Dutch cheese.

## Components

### Sous Vide French Rack of Venison

#### Ingredients

- 1 French rack of venison, about 570–580 g, with 5 ribs {scale}
- 4–5 g salt {scale}
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme {scale}
- 1 small sprig fresh rosemary {scale}
- 1 tbsp neutral oil for searing {scale}
- 20 g butter for searing {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Check the rack, remove any silver skin and pat dry. Rub with the salt and a little black pepper.
2. Place the whole rack, including the bones, in a vacuum bag with the thyme and rosemary. Protect sharp bone tips from puncturing the bag and seal carefully. Do not add oil or butter to the bag.
3. You can season and vacuum-seal the rack 1–3 hours ahead. Refrigerate at no more than 4 °C until cooking.
4. Preheat the water bath to 54.5 °C. Transfer the sealed rack directly from the refrigerator to the bath, keeping the meat fully submerged. Cook for 2 hours for a rack about 3 cm thick at its thickest meaty point.
5. This temperature gives a rosy result. It is not a validated pasteurisation process for game; if the source is not adequately controlled, or for vulnerable diners, cooking the meat fully is safer.
6. Remove the rack from the bag.
7. Pat the rack exceptionally dry with kitchen paper. Heat a heavy frying pan over high heat and add the neutral oil.
8. Sear the meaty sides for about 30–45 seconds per side until golden brown. Add the butter and briefly baste with the foaming butter.
9. Remove from the pan, rest for 2–3 minutes and cut between the ribs into chops. Keep searing brief to preserve the rosy centre.

### Silky Cauliflower Purée

#### Ingredients

- 300–400 g cauliflower {scale}
- 15–20 g butter {scale}
- Salt, to taste
- White or black pepper
- A tiny pinch of nutmeg
- A few drops of lemon juice, optional
- A little milk or cooking water, only if the purée is too thick

#### Instructions

1. Divide the cauliflower into small florets. You can also use the peeled, tender part of the stalk.
2. Boil or steam until completely tender, about 8–12 minutes depending on size. Drain very thoroughly and briefly allow excess moisture to steam off.
3. Transfer to a blender or tall blending jug. Add the butter and blend until as smooth as possible.
4. Season with salt, pepper, a tiny pinch of nutmeg and, if desired, a few drops of lemon juice.
5. The purée should be smooth and creamy but hold its shape on the plate. Do not use cream. Do not add milk or cooking water if already soft enough; if too thick, add warm cooking water just a teaspoon at a time.
6. For an extra fine texture, push through a fine sieve.
7. You can prepare the purée ahead. Cover and refrigerate if serving later. Reheat gently in a pan, stirring regularly, or in a food-safe, heat-resistant piping bag in warm water. Check that the centre is piping hot too.
8. Use a piping bag for a neat line or small dots, or make a sweep with the back of a spoon.

### Port and Game Jus with Juniper Berries

#### Ingredients

- 175 ml game stock {scale}
- 70 ml red port {scale}
- 1 small shallot or ¼ regular onion {scale}
- 2 lightly crushed juniper berries {scale}
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme {scale}
- 1 small sprig rosemary {scale}
- ¼–½ tsp soy sauce {scale}
- ½ tsp balsamic vinegar, approximately, to taste {scale}
- 15 g cold butter {scale}
- A small knob of butter for softening the onion
- Freshly ground black pepper
- A small splash of game stock or water for deglazing, optional

#### Instructions

1. Finely chop the shallot or onion. Melt a small knob of butter in a saucepan and soften the onion over medium-low heat for 2–3 minutes until translucent.
2. Add the red port, crushed juniper berries, thyme and rosemary. Reduce gently until about 30 ml remains, giving a concentrated, aromatic base.
3. Add the game stock, bring gently to the boil and simmer until reduced to about 100–150 ml of jus. For a lightly flowing jus, 150 ml is fine; reduce further for a stronger flavour.
4. Strain through a fine sieve and taste. If desired, add the soy sauce for extra umami, a few drops to about ½ teaspoon of balsamic vinegar to balance the sweet port, and black pepper. Go carefully with salt: game stock and soy sauce may already be quite salty.
5. Reheat gently just before serving. Taste again and reduce a little further if needed.
6. If desired, deglaze the venison pan with a small splash of game stock or water and add this to the jus before whisking in the butter.
7. Remove from the heat and whisk the ice-cold butter into the warm jus in small cubes. Do not let it boil vigorously afterwards. The jus should be glossy, full-flavoured and lightly coat a spoon.

### Thin, Crispy Oven Rösti

#### Ingredients

- 150 g raw potato {scale}
- ¼ small regular onion {scale}
- 10 g extra-mature Dutch cheese, finely grated {scale}
- ½ tsp fresh thyme leaves {scale}
- 1–2 tsp neutral oil {scale}
- A small pinch of salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- A little neutral oil for greasing the baking paper

#### Instructions

1. Peel and coarsely grate the potato. Finely grate the onion. Place both in a clean tea towel, twist shut and squeeze out as much moisture as possible.
2. Do not rinse the grated potato afterwards: the remaining potato starch helps bind the rösti.
3. Mix the squeezed potato and onion with the cheese, thyme, neutral oil, a small pinch of salt and black pepper. No butter or crème fraîche is needed.
4. Preheat the oven to 200 °C fan. Line a baking tray with baking paper and grease the paper very lightly.
5. Divide into two equal portions. Shape into 2 round rösti about 1–1.5 cm thick, pressing firmly so they hold together.
6. Bake for about 30–35 minutes at 1.5 cm thickness. Carefully turn after about 18–20 minutes once firm enough. Continue until golden on both sides and check with a small knife that the potato is soft and cooked through inside.
7. For thinner rösti about 7–8 mm thick, reduce the baking time to about 20–25 minutes.
8. You can fully bake the rösti ahead. Cool on a wire rack without covering while warm, otherwise they turn soft. Refrigerate once cool if not serving soon.
9. Just before serving, reheat for 5–7 minutes at 200 °C fan until hot and crisp again. Serve one rösti per person.

### Plating

#### Instructions

1. Use two large plates, preferably preheated. Make an elegant, elongated sweep of cauliflower purée on one side, or pipe a few neat dots.
2. Arrange 2 or 3 venison chops per person in a loose fan, with the ribs slightly raised and the rosy cut surfaces visible. Divide the 5 ribs between the plates according to chop size.
3. Place one golden rösti next to the meat. Keep it out of the purée and do not pour jus over it, so it stays crisp.
4. Spoon about 2–3 tablespoons of warm port and game jus around the meat. Serve any remaining jus separately in a small sauce jug.
5. Finish with a few small thyme leaves or a little freshly ground black pepper if desired. The creamy white purée, rosy venison, golden rösti and dark, glossy jus create a restaurant-style presentation.

### Key tips

- Venison: 54.5 °C for 2 hours at about 3 cm thickness gives a rosy result. Use no oil in the vacuum bag, pat exceptionally dry and sear very briefly over high heat.
- Cauliflower purée: start without milk or cream. Butter gives a soft, rounded flavour; keep the purée firm enough to hold its shape on the plate.
- Port jus: no red wine is needed. Port and game stock form the base; juniper, thyme and rosemary complement the game. Add soy sauce and balsamic sparingly and whisk in cold butter just before serving.
- Rösti: squeeze potato and onion thoroughly, use just 10 g of extra-mature cheese, bake until fully cooked and golden, and reheat until crisp just before serving.
