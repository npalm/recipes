A fresh, creamy dessert of buttermilk panna cotta, tender dried figs in orange syrup with thyme.

## Components

### Buttermilk panna cotta
prepTime: 10
cookTime: 5
waitTime: 360

#### Ingredients

- 150 ml buttermilk {scale}
- 100 ml whipping cream {scale}
- 25 g caster sugar {scale}
- 2 sheets gelatine {scale}
- 0.25 vanilla pod {scale}
- Small pinch of salt

#### Instructions

1. Place 2 sheets of gelatine in a large bowl of cold water. Soak for about 5 minutes.
2. Put 100 ml cream, 25 g sugar, the seeds from ¼ vanilla pod and a small pinch of salt in a saucepan. Heat gently to 65–70 °C. Do not boil.
3. Remove the pan from the heat. Squeeze the 2 gelatine sheets thoroughly and stir into the warm cream until completely dissolved.
4. Allow the cream mixture to cool to about 35–40 °C.
5. Slowly stir 150 ml cold buttermilk into the cream. Stir gently to avoid creating too many air bubbles.
6. Pour through a fine sieve for a smooth texture.
7. Divide between two silicone moulds of about 100–125 ml each. Pour any remaining mixture into a small glass.
8. Refrigerate for at least 6 hours, preferably overnight, until set. The panna cotta should be soft and gently wobbly while holding its shape when unmoulded.

### Poached figs with orange and thyme
prepTime: 5
cookTime: 25

#### Ingredients

- 4 dried figs (about 80 g) {scale}
- 100 ml fresh orange juice {scale}
- 50 ml water {scale}
- 10 g honey {scale}
- 1 sprig fresh thyme {scale}
- 2.5 ml balsamic vinegar (½ tsp) {scale}
- 5 ml lemon juice (1 tsp) {scale}

#### Instructions

1. Remove the tough stems from 4 dried figs (about 80 g). Cut lengthways in half to make 8 fig halves.
2. Put 100 ml orange juice, 50 ml water, 10 g honey, 1 sprig fresh thyme and 2.5 ml balsamic vinegar in a small saucepan.
3. Gently bring the mixture just below boiling point.
4. Add the 8 fig halves to the warm liquid, ensuring they are mostly submerged.
5. Poach gently over low heat for 15–20 minutes with the lid slightly ajar. The liquid may simmer very lightly but should not boil vigorously.
6. Test a fig with a fork. It should be soft and juicy while holding its shape. If still firm, poach for another 5 minutes.
7. Carefully lift the figs out with a spoon. Remove the thyme sprig.
8. Simmer the poaching liquid uncovered for a few minutes until lightly syrupy. It should run off a spoon rather than become as thick as caramel.
9. Remove the pan from the heat and stir 5 ml lemon juice into the sauce.
10. Return the figs to the syrup and keep lukewarm until serving. If preparing ahead, allow to cool and refrigerate covered in their syrup. Reheat gently over low heat just before serving.

### Plating

#### Ingredients

- A few tiny fresh thyme leaves, optional

#### Instructions

1. Take the panna cotta out of the fridge. Gently loosen the edges of the silicone moulds and turn out onto two chilled dessert plates.
2. Place 4 poached fig halves per person beside the panna cotta. The figs may be lukewarm but should not be hot.
3. Spoon about 1–2 teaspoons of orange-fig syrup over and around the figs.
4. Garnish with a few tiny fresh thyme leaves if desired.

## Notes

- Allow about 40 minutes of active work and at least 6 hours of chilling for the panna cotta. Most components can be prepared ahead.
- The day before: make and chill the panna cotta.
- The day before or earlier in the evening: poach the dried figs and refrigerate covered in their syrup.
- 10 minutes before dessert: gently warm the figs in their syrup.
- Just before serving: unmould the panna cotta and plate the dessert.
- Taste the fig syrup before serving. Dried figs are already sweet. If the sauce tastes heavy or overly syrupy, add a splash of water and a few drops of lemon juice.
